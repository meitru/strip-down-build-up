# strip-down-build-up
A public log of one year spent proving I can do hard things. This isn't a productivity system to admire, but a record of whether I actually did it.

Why this exists

Three things I want, in order of how much they scare me:

    Proof. I want evidence, in my own handwriting, that I can do hard things.
    Enjoyment. I want to actually use the hobbies I already own things for.
    Calm. I want a productivity system so easy to follow that it stops being a decision.

The bet underneath all three: order is the art. Simplify the routine, the home, the stuff, until following it costs nothing. Then the hard thing has room to happen.

The rules (2026 → onward)

These are the behaviours the whole year rests on. Four nos and two yeses.

What I stop doing

    Not wasting time scrolling. No unfocused feed time.
    Not buying things I don't need. One in, one out.
    Not re-deciding things I've already decided.
    Not buying my way out of a problem I could solve by simplifying.

What I do

    Move my body every day. Non-negotiable, any duration, any form.
    Spend real time in my hobbies — not researching them, doing them.

What I learn

    Casually. Curiosity over curriculum. No course I have to finish.

Goals for 2027
1. Pay down debt

    Pay off the BAC Platinum card by end of year.

2. Save money

    Use up every existing skincare product before buying another.
    Save $5,000 across 2027.

3. Improve health

    Work out daily.

What actually makes these work

Every 2027 goal runs on the same machine. If the machine runs, the goals are almost automatic:

    One fixed slot. The workout, the no-spend check, and the log entry happen at a time of day that never moves. Decided once, in December 2026.
    The floor, not the ceiling. A 10-minute walk counts. A $20 transfer counts. A one-line log entry counts. The streak is the asset — never break it for pride.
    Money and stuff get one dashboard. Skincare inventory, card balance, savings progress. One place, updated weekly. What isn't visible isn't real.
    The repo is the record, not the plan. Plan lives in one page. Everything else here is what happened.

Monthly review

First weekend of the month. Fifteen minutes, longer if it's a good conversation with myself.

    Add up the weekly numbers.
    Card balance — am I on pace for payoff before December?
    Savings — am I on pace for $5,000?
    Skincare shelf: is it visibly emptier than 30 days ago?
    Any purchase I regret? Note it, don't punish it.
    Did anything get complicated this month? Strip it out.

The house rule

If this repo starts feeling like a project to maintain, I've failed at the thing I set out to do. Strip it out. Keep the log.

The point was never the system. The point was to find out what I do when the system gets out of the way.
